Opportunity cost

Expected annual after-tax return on money you could otherwise invest. The model carries payments and refunds to each option’s end date. This is an assumption, not guaranteed income; use zero to ignore it.

What the money paid for the car could earn elsewhere after tax. Earlier payments tie up cash for longer; VAT refunds release it.

Opportunity cost is measured at each option’s end date. A 0% return removes opportunity cost. The results below show its contribution separately.

How the timing calculation works

For each payment or receipt: amount × [(1 + annual return)months remaining / 12 − 1]. Receipts use negative amounts. Refunds after the comparison end are discounted back to that date.

VAT refunds and sale tax

Refunds reduce cost. Sale VAT is collected within the buyer’s payment and then paid to the tax authority.

Enter prices including VAT, except a lease explicitly marked excluding VAT. Results subtract eligible VAT refunds and include disposal VAT when VAT registration is enabled. Income tax, private-use adjustments and fuel or charging are excluded. Partial VAT recovery is a planning approximation.

The balloon loan assumes equal monthly payments with the balloon due alongside the final regular payment. The standard loan has no final outstanding balance. Each loan has its own fixed interest rate. Buying outright pays the shared purchase price at month zero, then follows the non-lease sell or keep choice at its selected end date. The default period is entered in whole months. Uncheck matching periods to set individual terms and resale estimates; monthly economic costs then determine rankings when terms differ. Resale is your estimate, not a guaranteed future value. A kept car is credited as an asset at estimated resale value net of disposal VAT, so it is not treated as cash received.

Opportunity cost carries each cash outflow and inflow to that option’s end date at your annual after-tax return. A refund three months after buyout is included and discounted back to the end date. This return is an assumption, not a guaranteed investment. Maintenance assumes steady mileage, using whichever interval comes first. A service due exactly at the end is included. Default seasonal costs cover six evenly spaced changes and storage periods, starting at purchase. VAT on eligible maintenance and tyres is netted against the expense immediately in this comparison, treating next-month deductibility as settled. It creates no outstanding VAT at the end. Lease VAT follows its separate delay, defaulting to deduction with each payment. The buyer pays the VAT-inclusive resale price at the end of the term. Its VAT component is then paid to the tax authority at that same modelled date, leaving the VAT-exclusive proceeds. This tax payment is shown separately and is not a refund. The capital VAT cap applies separately to a outright or loan-funded purchase, or lease buyout.
